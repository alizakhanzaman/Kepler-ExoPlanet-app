Project

About

A university lab and research project by Aliza Zaman (BSSE). It replicates a published exoplanet classifier, benchmarks it properly, and extends it from detection to prioritisation of unresolved Kepler candidates.

Summary

Data: the NASA Kepler cumulative KOI table (Kaggle snapshot, 9,564 signals). Model: a scikit-learn pipeline with median imputation, standard scaling and LightGBM, trained on the 7,316 labelled signals with the base paper's 11 features. The 2,248 CANDIDATE signals are never used for training; they are scored and ranked.

Source code, workflows and research scripts: github.com/alizakhanzaman/Kepler-ExoPlanet-app.

Ethics and intended use

This is a teaching and research prototype. Its output is not a validated astronomical classification and should not be cited as a discovery or used to claim that any object is habitable. All data are public; no personal data are collected, and the site sets no cookies and loads no third-party scripts or fonts.

Limitations and threats to validity

  • Catalogue values, not light curves. The model sees fitted parameters from the Kepler pipeline, so it inherits that pipeline's errors and cannot spot problems visible only in the raw signal.
  • Labels and selection effects. CONFIRMED and FALSE POSITIVE labels come from NASA vetting; signals that were easy to resolve are over-represented among labelled rows, so CANDIDATE rows may differ from the training distribution.
  • ESS is not habitability. It compares radius and insolation with Earth's only. Atmosphere, mass, orbit eccentricity and stellar activity are ignored, and its weighting is a design choice (sensitivity is reported).
  • Uncalibrated probabilities. p_planet is a model score; calibration is planned, not done.
  • NASA check is not independent. Candidates resolved since the snapshot were not chosen at random, and NASA's vetting uses related signals, so agreement is supportive rather than proof.
  • Replication assumptions. The base paper does not state its split ratio, seed or scaling; the choices made here are listed on the Results page.
  • Demo model uses 11 features. The 36-feature model scores higher in cross-validation but needs inputs a user cannot type by hand.
